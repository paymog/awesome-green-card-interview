# Awesome Green Card Interview [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> Resources for couples who prepare for a marriage-based green card (adjustment of status) interview at a USCIS field office.

The maintained copy of this list is at [readyformyinterview.com/resources](https://readyformyinterview.com/resources). This README is generated from it.

This is a list of useful places for couples who prepare for a marriage-based green card interview at a USCIS field office. It covers the official pages, free and paid legal help, documents and translations, forums, filing tools, and interview preparation. Each entry says what the resource is good for. We last checked every link on November 15, 2026.

Ready for My Interview made this list. We also make a practice tool, and four of our own pages are in the interview preparation group. Everything else is from other people. No one paid to be on this list, and there are no affiliate links. Entries that charge money say "paid". This list is not legal advice. It cannot tell you which form to file, what your officer will ask, or what USCIS will decide.

Start with the official pages. USCIS forms, fees, and rules change, and the USCIS page is the one that counts. Read the instructions for each form you file, and read your interview notice from start to end. A summary on another site, including this one, can be out of date.

Use the other groups for the work around the forms. Legal help is for questions about your own case. Document help is for records that you must order or translate. Forums show what other couples saw, but one story is not a rule. Filing tools help you fill in forms for a fee. Interview preparation is for the weeks before the appointment.

Keep your private numbers private. Do not post your A-number, receipt number, passport number, or home address in a forum or in a public comment. If a site asks for money to "guarantee" a result or to "speed up" a case, stop and read the USCIS page on scams first.

## Official USCIS pages

These pages come from U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services. They are free. When a USCIS page and another site disagree, follow USCIS and your notices.

- [Green card for immediate relatives of a U.S. citizen](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/green-card-for-immediate-relatives-of-us-citizen) - The USCIS overview for a spouse of a U.S. citizen. It says who qualifies and which forms the process uses.
- [Adjustment of status](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/adjustment-of-status) - How to apply for a green card from inside the United States. Read it to check that adjustment, not a visa abroad, is your path.
- [All USCIS forms](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms) - Every current form, with its instructions and filing addresses. Always download a form here, so you have the current edition.
- [Form I-130](https://www.uscis.gov/i-130) - The petition the U.S. citizen or permanent resident spouse files. The page has the form, the instructions, and the evidence list for a spouse.
- [Form I-485](https://www.uscis.gov/i-485) - The application to adjust status. The page has the form, the instructions, and the list of documents to send with it.
- [Form I-864](https://www.uscis.gov/i-864) - The affidavit of support. It shows that the sponsor can support the applicant. The instructions say which income and tax evidence to send.
- [Form I-765](https://www.uscis.gov/i-765) - The work permit application. Many applicants file it with Form I-485, so they can work while the case is open.
- [Form I-131](https://www.uscis.gov/i-131) - The travel document application. Read it before you plan a trip outside the United States while your Form I-485 is open.
- [Fee schedule (Form G-1055)](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1055) - The official fee for each form. Check it on the day you file, because fees change.
- [Fee calculator](https://www.uscis.gov/feecalculator) - Answers a few questions and shows the fee for one form. Use it to check a total before you write a check or pay online.
- [File online](https://www.uscis.gov/file-online) - Shows which forms you can file online and how to start. Online filing gives you notices in your account.
- [USCIS online account](https://my.uscis.gov/) - Where you file online, read notices, and see case updates. Both spouses can have their own account.
- [Processing times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/) - The time USCIS takes for most cases of a form type at each office. It is a range, not a promise for your case.
- [Case status online](https://egov.uscis.gov/) - Type a receipt number to see the last action on a case. Use it to check that a notice is real.
- [Field office locator](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/find-a-uscis-office/field-offices) - Find the USCIS field office that serves your address. Your interview notice names the office, so check the address there too.
- [Change of address](https://www.uscis.gov/addresschange) - Report a new address to USCIS. A notice sent to an old address can cost you an appointment.
- [USCIS Contact Center](https://www.uscis.gov/contactcenter) - Ask about a case, ask for a disability accommodation, or ask to reschedule an appointment. Have your receipt number ready.
- [Form I-693](https://www.uscis.gov/i-693) - The medical exam report. A civil surgeon fills it in. The page says when to submit it.
- [Find a civil surgeon](https://www.uscis.gov/tools/find-a-civil-surgeon) - Search for a doctor who USCIS has designated to do the medical exam. You can filter by language.
- [Request your records (FOIA)](https://www.uscis.gov/records/request-records-through-the-freedom-of-information-act-or-privacy-act) - Ask USCIS for a copy of your own immigration file. Some applicants use it to check what they filed in an earlier case.
- [Avoid scams](https://www.uscis.gov/avoid-scams) - Common immigration scams, including fake lawyers and notarios, and how to report them. Read it before you pay anyone.

## Free and low-cost legal help

Only a lawyer or a DOJ-accredited representative at a recognized organization can give you legal advice about your case. A notario, a form preparer, or a friend cannot. These directories help you find real help. Many nonprofits charge a low fee or nothing.

- [USCIS: find legal services](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/legal-services) - How to tell a lawyer or accredited representative from someone who is not allowed to give legal advice, and where to look for free help.
- [DOJ recognized organizations and accredited representatives](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-accreditation-roster-reports) - The Justice Department rosters of nonprofits it recognizes and the accredited representatives who give immigration legal help through them, by state and city.
- [Immigration Advocates Network legal directory](https://www.immigrationadvocates.org/legaldirectory/) - Search nonprofit immigration legal services by location, language, and type of case.
- [CLINIC](https://www.cliniclegal.org/) - A national network of nonprofit immigration legal programs. Its site helps you find an affiliate program near you.
- [ILRC community resources](https://www.ilrc.org/community-resources) - Plain-language handouts from the Immigrant Legal Resource Center, a nonprofit. Many are in more than one language.
- [LawHelp.org](https://www.lawhelp.org/) - Free legal aid offices and self-help pages by state. It helps with problems next to the case, such as housing or family law.
- [AILA lawyer search](https://ailalawyer.com/) - Paid. Search for immigration lawyers who are members of the American Immigration Lawyers Association. Lawyers set their own fees, so ask about cost at the first call.

If a lawyer helps with your case, tell the lawyer about your interview date as soon as you have it. Ask what the lawyer wants you to bring, and whether the lawyer will attend. The guide to [working with a lawyer](https://readyformyinterview.com/lawyer-prep) explains what a lawyer does before the interview.

## Documents and translations

Most of the work before an interview is records: certificates, tax papers, leases, and photos. Other agencies hold many of them. Order official records early, because some offices take weeks and some charge a fee.

- [Reciprocity and civil documents by country](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country.html) - The State Department list of the birth, marriage, divorce, and police records each country issues, and how to get them. Some countries have no birth certificate, and the page says what to use instead.
- [Where to write for vital records](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/w2w/index.htm) - The office in each U.S. state that issues birth, death, marriage, and divorce records, with fees and addresses.
- [IRS tax transcripts](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript) - Order free tax return transcripts. A sponsor often uses them as tax evidence for Form I-864.
- [I-94 arrival record](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/) - Get your most recent arrival record from Customs and Border Protection. Form I-485 asks about your last entry.
- [American Translators Association directory](https://www.atanet.org/client-assistance/find-translator-interpreter-near-you) - Paid. Find a professional translator by language and location. Translators set their own prices.
- [8 CFR 103.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/subpart-A/section-103.2) - The federal rule on how to submit evidence. A foreign-language document needs a full English translation. The translator certifies that the translation is complete and accurate, and that they can translate into English.

Bring originals of the documents you sent copies of, unless your notice says something else. Put them in the order of your notice, so you can find a page while the officer waits. If a record and your memory disagree, do not change your answer to match. Ask your lawyer.

## Forums and communities

Forums are good for one question: what did other couples see? They are not good for legal questions about your case. Offices differ, and officers differ. A post from two years ago may describe a rule that has changed. Before you post, remove names, dates of birth, and every case number.

- [r/immigration](https://www.reddit.com/r/immigration/) - A large Reddit community for U.S. immigration questions. Read its rules before you post. It does not allow advertising.
- [r/USCIS](https://www.reddit.com/r/USCIS/) - A Reddit community for USCIS process questions, such as notices, biometrics, and interview scheduling.
- [VisaJourney](https://www.visajourney.com/) - A long-running forum with case timelines that members share. Search for your field office to read interview reports from other couples.

## Filing tools

These companies help you prepare the forms for a fee. They are not USCIS. Check what a fee includes, whether a lawyer reviews the forms, and what happens if USCIS sends a request for evidence. You can also file some forms yourself, for free, from your USCIS online account. The government filing fee is separate from any company fee.

- [CitizenPath](https://citizenpath.com/) - Paid. Online preparation of USCIS forms, including family-based forms. Individuals, lawyers, and nonprofits use it. Its marriage green card guide is free to read.
- [Boundless](https://www.boundless.com/) - Paid. Software and support from an in-house team, including lawyers, to prepare a family-based green card application.
- [SimpleCitizen](https://www.simplecitizen.com/) - Paid. Builds your forms from the documents and details you give. The site says an independent attorney reviews the application.

## Interview preparation

The interview is a conversation about your forms and your life together. The officer may check facts on your forms and ask about your relationship. These resources explain how the interview works and help you practice your own answers. Do not memorize a script.

- [USCIS Policy Manual: interview guidelines](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-7-part-a-chapter-5) - The official chapter on adjustment interviews. It covers who must appear, interpreters, and what the officer reviews.
- [USCIS Policy Manual: spouses](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-b-chapter-6) - How USCIS looks at a marriage-based petition, including what a bona fide marriage means.
- [Form G-1256](https://www.uscis.gov/g-1256) - The interpreter declaration. Read it before the interview if you bring an interpreter. You sign it in front of the officer, not before.
- [CitizenPath: green card interview questions](https://citizenpath.com/green-card-interview-questions/) - A free article with common questions by topic and a practice plan.
- [Boundless: preparing for the marriage green card interview](https://www.boundless.com/immigration-resources/preparing-for-the-marriage-green-card-interview) - A free article on what to expect, what to bring, and how to get ready together.
- [Printable interview checklist](https://readyformyinterview.com/green-card-interview-checklist) - Our one-sheet list of documents to bring and questions to practice. It is free, and it needs no email.
- [All 91 interview questions](https://readyformyinterview.com/questions/) - Our practice questions, grouped by topic. Each one has a page that says what the officer may check and which follow-up questions to expect.
- [USCIS field offices](https://readyformyinterview.com/field-offices/) - Our list of every field office with its address and the areas it serves.
- [Stokes interview guide](https://readyformyinterview.com/stokes-interview) - Our guide to separate questioning of spouses, and how to prepare honestly together and apart.

A good way to use these resources: read the official chapter once, then pick one topic, such as how you met. Each spouse answers out loud, alone, from memory. Then check the facts against your records. Repeat for the next topic. Answers do not need to match word for word. Two people remember one day in different ways.

## After the interview

- [Conditional permanent residence](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/after-we-grant-your-green-card/conditional-permanent-residence) - If you were married less than two years when you got your green card, it is conditional. This page explains what that means.
- [Form I-751](https://www.uscis.gov/i-751) - The petition to remove conditions. Read it early, so you know which joint records to keep over the next two years.
